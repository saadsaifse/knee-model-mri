Running while you rehab

  • Raise your cadence 5–10% at the same pace (a metronome app helps). It shortens the stride and lowers kneecap load per step by roughly 10–15%.
  • Cut volume before cutting runs. Shorter, flatter and softer surfaces first, and avoid downhills and deep stairs for now: they combine high bend with high load.
  • Use the same pain rule: ≤ 3/10 during the run, settled by the next morning.

When to go back to the doctor

This page is an educational model built from your report. It is not a diagnosis or a treatment plan, and the numbers are estimates for a typical 75 kg person, not measurements of your knee. Your referral mentioned locking and decreased range of movement. The MRI found no mechanical cause (no meniscal tear, no loose body), and kneecap catching often feels like locking, but go back to your doctor or an orthopaedic surgeon promptly if the knee truly locks (gets stuck and you can't straighten it), gives way, swells up, or you can't bear weight. A physiotherapist can check your hip and quadriceps control in person, tape the kneecap if that helps, and progress these exercises safely.
