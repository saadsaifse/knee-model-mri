Your left knee, part 2: cycling, the gym, the cartilage crack and the marathon

This page builds on the first one (knee.html): same MRI, same model, three new questions. In short, the MRI shows a kneecap that sits and rides outward in a shallow-at-the-top groove (8 mm shift, TT-TG 17 mm), mildly irritated quadriceps and patellar tendons, and a tiny crack in the cartilage of the kneecap's outer facet with some irritated bone under it. Nothing is torn.

The bench now has Cycle and Cycle, low saddle, and travels to 120° of bend, because cycling works the knee deeper than running does. The model is also slightly refined: near a straight knee the kneecap is now still pressed lightly into its groove, as it is in reality, which raises the walking and straight-leg numbers a little. Running is unchanged.
