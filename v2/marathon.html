The December marathon

You have about six weeks. Your pain is falling while you train, which is the most important fact here. Running the marathon is reasonable if three things are true: you can already run about 20 km or more without pain going above 3/10, the knee doesn't swell afterwards, and it's no worse the next morning. If one of those fails, the honest options are run-walking the whole race at an easier pace, dropping to the half, or deferring. Six weeks is too short to build both mileage and kneecap tolerance from scratch.

What the model says matters most for race day is the per-landing peak, so the cheapest wins are a quicker cadence, a run-walk rhythm from the start (walk breaks give the facet and tendon a pause before fatigue sets in and the knee starts to drift inward), and flat, even pacing, because downhills combine a deep bend with braking.
