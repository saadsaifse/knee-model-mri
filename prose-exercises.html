What the bench shows when you walk and run

  • Walking loads the kneecap lightly (about 0.4× body weight). But the knee is nearly straight for most of the step, so the groove barely holds the kneecap and it rides outward by up to ~8 mm. That shallow, off-centre ride can produce the clicking, catching and "giving" feelings people often describe as locking.
  • Running is the opposite. At about 40° of bend, when your foot is under you, the groove has the kneecap seated, but the joint carries about 4–5× body weight. Most of that lands on the outer facet, where the cartilage crack is: the model peaks near 11 MPa. The same moment loads the patellar tendon at about 5× body weight, which is why both tendons are irritated.
  • Stairs sit in between, at about 60° and 2× body weight.

Rehab can't move bone, so TT-TG stays 17 mm. It changes three things on the bench. Stronger, better-timed quadriceps stiffen the inner spring. Hip strength stops the knee caving inward under load, which otherwise adds an extra few millimetres of outward pull at the anchor. A slightly quicker running cadence lowers the peak load on each step. Toggle After rehab to see all three together.

How to dose it. Mild discomfort up to about 3/10 during an exercise is fine, as long as it settles within 24 hours and isn't worse the next morning. If it is, drop the load or depth for the next session rather than stopping. Start every exercise below at the easy end, and expect 8–12 weeks of steady work before running feels normal.
