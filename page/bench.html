What the bench shows when you walk and run

  • Walking loads the kneecap lightly (about 0.6× body weight). But the knee is nearly straight for most of the step, so the groove barely holds the kneecap and it rides outward by about 8 mm. That shallow, off-centre ride can produce the clicking, catching and "giving" feelings people often describe as locking.
  • Running is the opposite. At about 40° of bend, when your foot is under you, the groove has the kneecap seated, but the joint carries about 4.4× body weight. Most of that lands on the outer facet, where the cartilage crack is: the model peaks near 11 MPa. The same moment loads the patellar tendon at about 5× body weight, which is why both tendons are irritated.
  • Stairs sit in between, at about 55° and 2.3× body weight.

Rehab can't move bone, so TT-TG stays 17 mm. It changes three things on the bench. Stronger, better-timed quadriceps stiffen the inner spring. Hip strength stops the knee caving inward under load, which otherwise adds an extra few millimetres of outward pull at the anchor. A slightly quicker running cadence lowers the peak load on each step. Toggle After rehab to see all three together.
